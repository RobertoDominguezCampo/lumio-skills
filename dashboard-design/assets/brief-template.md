# Brief template

Ask these questions **before you design**. A dashboard without a brief is a guess.
Write the answers at the top of the prototype task, in this order.

| # | Question | What to write | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Audience**: who reads it? | One role, as specific as possible: their job, not their department | The fleet manager of the Fernhill store |
| 2 | **Decision**: what do they decide with it, and how often? | A verb + an object + a rhythm | Every week, which bikes to have ready so nothing sells out at peak hours |
| 3 | **First question**: what do they ask first when they open it? | One question, in their words | What runs out, and when? |
| 4 | **Never**: what must the page not do? | The trap to avoid | Show numbers they can't act on |

## How the answers shape the design

- **Audience** sets the vocabulary, the level of detail and the filters.
- **Decision** sets the period and the controls (a weekly decision → weekly data, a week
  or 4-week toggle).
- **First question** becomes the **page title**, written as its answer in one sentence.
- **Never** is the cut list: every element must help the decision, or it goes.
- **One decision ending in one action** → use the **Executive Z insight variant, one-page
  version** (`layouts/executive-z.md`): KPI strip, main chart 2/3, action card 1/3, no table,
  no side navigation.
- **Several decisions or exploration** → choose the layout with the normal rules (SKILL.md,
  Rule 2).

## Checklist before you hand over

- [ ] The title answers question 3.
- [ ] Every number on the page helps the decision in question 2.
- [ ] Nothing on the page breaks question 4.
- [ ] The action card says what to do in this period and what it is worth.
